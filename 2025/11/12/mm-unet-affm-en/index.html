AFFM: deciding what to trust before fusing

发表于2025-11-12|更新于2026-10-11|English Notes
|浏览量:

5 min read

中文版：AFFM：融合之前，先决定该相信什么

Detailed notes on MM-UNet’s Adaptive Feature Fusion Module.

The problem it addresses

The conventional skip connection concatenates high-level and low-level features directly. That means:

  • Background noise from the shallow layers mixes into genuinely useful information.
  • The final boundary becomes less precise, not more.

Two maps, two characters

AFFM behaves like a careful cartographer merging two maps.

Low-level features High-level features
Think of it as A detail map An outline map
Strength Rich edges and texture Knows the overall shape of the prostate
Weakness Heavy background noise, no global sense No precise localisation

How it works, in three steps

flowchart TD
  A[High-level features from the decoder] --> B[Upsample to match size]
  C[Low-level features from the encoder] --> D[Concatenate along channels]
  B --> D
  D --> E[Global average pooling]
  E --> F[Two fully connected layers with ReLU]
  F --> H[Sigmoid gives a weight per channel]
  H --> I[Multiply the low-level features]
  C --> I
  I --> J[Add the aligned high-level features]
  B --> J
  J --> K[Fused output]

Step one — align and concatenate

Upsampling brings the high-level map to the same spatial size, so the two can be combined at one scale.

Step two — compute the weights

  • Global average pooling reduces each channel to a single number — a summary of how strongly that channel responded overall.
  • Two fully connected layers learn the relationships between channels.
  • Sigmoid squashes the result into 0–1.

α ≈ 1 means keep this channel. α ≈ 0 means suppress it.

Step three — weighted fusion

Two design choices worth pausing on:

Why weight only the low-level features? They are the mixed bag — full of both useful edges and noise, so they are what needs filtering. The high-level features are already comparatively clean and carry the semantic meaning; reweighting them risks distorting information the network worked hard to build.

Why add instead of concatenate? Addition is cheaper, introduces no extra parameters, and combines the two signals naturally. Concatenation would grow the channel count and require another convolution to reduce it again.

Why it is a good module

  1. Noise suppression is learned, not specified. Nobody writes a rule for which channels are noisy; the network works it out.
  2. Selection rather than accumulation. It is not “use everything and hope”, it is “decide, then use”.
  3. Trained end to end. The attention weights are optimised by the same loss as everything else.

Where it sits in MM-UNet

AFFM runs at every decoder stage, right after upsampling:

Applied layer by layer, this keeps fine detail available in the early stages while letting deep semantics fill in the global picture — which is what precise boundary localisation actually needs.

The one-line version

AFFM is not feature concatenation with extra steps. It is the network learning what to look at before it looks — and cleaning only the input that needs cleaning, while leaving the already-good input alone.
