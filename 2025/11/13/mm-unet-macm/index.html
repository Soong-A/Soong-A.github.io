MM-UNet MACM 模块详解：多尺度各向异性卷积如何提取 3D 特征

发表于2025-11-13|更新于2026-10-22|MM-UNet 论文精读
MM-UNet 论文精读 · 6/6专题目录

阅读约 7 分钟

专题：MM-UNet 论文精读。前置知识：参数量计算、GFLOPs 计算、U-Net 结构详解。相关章节：AFFM、GCM。

3D MRI 并不是由完全等距的立方体组成。前列腺 MR 常见的情况是：切片平面内分辨率较高，切片之间的间距却更大。MACM 因此不使用一个各方向完全相同的立方卷积核，而是把平面内信息与跨切片信息分开处理。

模块先做多尺度，再做融合

flowchart TD
  A[输入特征] --> B[1x1x1 卷积调整通道]
  B --> C[ACB：k 等于 3]
  B --> D[ACB：k 等于 7]
  B --> E[ACB：k 等于 13]
  B --> F[ACB：k 等于 15]
  B --> G[拼接调整后的输入与所有分支输出]
  C --> G
  D --> G
  E --> G
  F --> G
  G --> H[1x1x1 卷积融合并设置输出通道]
  H --> I[输出特征]

这不是把原输入与多尺度结果做一次简单残差相加。论文描述的是把输入与四个 ACB 分支的结果连接起来，再用 1×1×1 卷积完成融合与通道整理。这样，每个尺度的信息都被显式保留下来。

小卷积核偏向局部边缘和纹理，大卷积核覆盖更宽的器官形状与邻近结构。四个尺度同时存在，是为了避免只用一个固定感受野来解释大小和形态会变化的前列腺。

一个 ACB 里有两个方向

flowchart TD
  A[ACB 输入] --> B[1x1xk 卷积：沿切片方向]
  A --> C[kxkx1 卷积：在切片平面内]
  B --> D[两条分支逐元素相加]
  C --> D
  D --> E[ACB 输出]

1×1×k：跨切片

这一支只沿深度轴展开，重点观察相邻切片之间的连续变化。它帮助网络追踪前列腺从底部到顶部时形状如何改变，也能减少相邻切片预测边界忽然跳动的问题。

k×k×1：切片内

这一支在单张切片的平面上扩大感受野，关注局部纹理、轮廓以及前列腺与周围组织的关系。深度方向保持为 1，因此不会把粗糙的层间分辨率当成与平面内完全相同。

两条支路输出尺寸一致，所以可以相加。这里的“各向异性”不是一个装饰性名词，而是把 MRI 三个方向不同的物理间距直接反映到卷积核形状中。

为什么大卷积核还可以接受

直接使用 k×k×k 卷积时，单个输入—输出通道对需要 k³ 个权重。把不同方向拆开后，成本可以显著降低。

形式 核的结构 单通道参数量的量级
标准 3D 卷积 k×k×k k³
各向异性双分支 1×1×k 与 k×k×1 k + k²
三轴分解 k×1×1、1×k×1、1×1×k 3k

论文还讨论了把大 3D 卷积分解为沿三个轴的卷积组合。要注意：3k 是这种三轴分解的参数量，不应直接拿来描述上面 ACB 的两条并行分支。把这两件事分开，计算逻辑才不会混乱。

MACM 在 MM-UNet 里做什么

AFFM 先筛选并融合编码器细节与解码器语义；MACM 随后在多个尺度上整理这份融合特征，让它同时满足两种需求：在切片内精确定位边界，在切片之间保持三维连续性。

我会用下面的顺序记住它：

  1. 用 1×1×1 卷积控制通道。
  2. 把同一输入送入四个不同尺度的 ACB。
  3. 每个 ACB 再把平面内与跨切片信息分开建模。
  4. 拼接原输入与四个尺度的输出。
  5. 用最后的 1×1×1 卷积完成融合。

MACM 的核心不是“卷积核越大越好”，而是让卷积核的方向与尺度都对应真实的体数据几何。

Notes on MM-UNet’s Multi-scale Anisotropic Convolution Module — a feature extractor designed specifically for 3D medical volumes.

What it is for

MACM targets three problems at once:

  • Anisotropic resolution — spacing differs between axes.
  • Multi-scale demand — local detail and global context both matter.
  • Computational cost — performance cannot come at any price.

Structure

flowchart TD
  A[Input features] --> B[1x1x1 convolution adjusts channels]
  B --> C[ACB with k equals 3]
  B --> D[ACB with k equals 7]
  B --> E[ACB with k equals 13]
  B --> F[ACB with k equals 15]
  B --> G[Concatenate input and all branch outputs]
  C --> G
  D --> G
  E --> G
  F --> G
  G --> H[1x1x1 convolution adjusts output channels]
  H --> I[Output features]
  1. A 1×1×1 convolution sets the channel count.
  2. Four anisotropic convolution blocks process the features in parallel at different scales.
  3. The adjusted input and the four branch outputs are concatenated along the channel axis.
  4. A final 1×1×1 convolution fuses the result and sets the output channels.

Inside an ACB

flowchart TD
  A[ACB input] --> B[1x1xk convolution: across slices]
  A --> C[kxkx1 convolution: within the plane]
  B --> D[Add the two branches]
  C --> D
  D --> E[ACB output]

Branch one — 1×1×k, across slices

Handles relationships along the z axis: continuity between consecutive slices, and three-dimensional context within the volume.

In prostate segmentation this is what tracks the gland’s changing shape from base to apex, follows the urethra through multiple slices, and keeps the boundary consistent from one slice to the next.

Branch two — k×k×1, within the plane

Handles spatial patterns inside a single slice: texture, boundary contour, local anatomical structure.

This is what detects the gland outline precisely, distinguishes internal tissue heterogeneity, and picks up local features such as cysts and calcifications.

Combining them

Addition is cheap, adds no parameters, and lets gradients flow cleanly through both branches.

The cost argument

A standard 3D convolution has O(k³) parameters, growing cubically with kernel size. MACM factorises it into a 1×1×k and a k×k×1 branch.

Kernel type Parameters At k = 7
Standard 3D k³ 343
Factorised ACB k + k² 56
Reduction 1 − (k + k²) / k³ 84%

The factorisation works because feature space is approximately separable, directional information is relatively independent, and medical images are structurally regular enough for that assumption to hold.

Why four different kernel sizes

Kernel Receptive field Main role Where it helps
k = 3 Local Fine detail Precise boundary placement
k = 7 Medium Structural features Internal tissue analysis
k = 13 Large Context modelling Relationships between organs
k = 15 Global Whole-shape context Overall morphology

Running them in parallel means the scale coverage is complete — from pixel-level detail to organ-level structure — and the module stays robust when target size varies.

Fit to prostate MR specifically

In-plane resolution is roughly 0.5–1.0 mm; slice thickness is 3–5 mm. The ACB split matches that asymmetry directly rather than pretending the voxels are cubes.

  • Small kernel (k = 3) — detects subtle boundary changes, identifies cysts and small calcifications.
  • Medium kernel (k = 7) — analyses internal gland structure, separates the central zone from the peripheral zone.
  • Large kernels (k = 13, 15) — capture the spatial relationship with bladder and rectum, and the overall shape and position.

The thing I found most instructive: the module’s design is not a general improvement borrowed from elsewhere. Every choice in it is traceable back to a physical property of the scanner output.
