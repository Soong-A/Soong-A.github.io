U-Net 结构详解：编码器、跳跃连接与解码器如何做医学图像分割

发表于2026-10-22|更新于2026-10-25|医学影像

阅读约 7 分钟

做医学图像分割，几乎绕不开 U-Net。它用对称的编码器—解码器和跳跃连接，把全局语义与像素级定位结合起来，后来成为医学图像分割最具代表性的基础架构之一。MM-UNet 以及许多 CNN、Transformer 分割模型，都沿用了或改造了这种 U 形思路。

它要解决什么问题

分割要求为每个像素输出类别，需要同时满足两件互相冲突的事：

  1. 知道这是什么：需要深层的、感受野很大的抽象语义特征。
  2. 知道边界在哪：需要浅层的、分辨率很高的细节特征。

分类网络一路下采样，丢掉了位置细节；只用高分辨率特征，又看不到整体。U-Net 的答案是：先压缩拿语义，再放大恢复分辨率，并且把压缩之前的细节通过”捷径”直接送回来。

整体结构

紫色路径压缩空间并提取语义，绿色路径恢复分辨率；金色连线把同尺度的边界细节直接送入解码器。

三个部分

编码器（收缩路径）

每一级由两个 3x3 卷积加 ReLU 组成，随后用 2x2 最大池化把空间尺寸减半，同时把通道数翻倍。原论文图 1 明确给出了 64、128、256、512、1024 五级通道数，也就是连续下采样 4 次。

随着分辨率下降，每个位置的感受野越来越大，表达的信息也从边缘、纹理逐渐变成器官和区域。

解码器（扩张路径）

每一级先用上采样（原论文是 2x2 转置卷积）把分辨率翻倍、通道数减半，再和编码器同尺度特征拼接，最后经过两个 3x3 卷积。

跳跃连接

这是 U-Net 最关键的设计。编码器第 k 级的输出，不仅送给下一级继续压缩，也直接送到解码器同尺度的位置，沿通道维度拼接：

$$\text{decoder input} = \mathrm{concat}\bigl( \mathrm{upsample}(F_{\text{deep}}),\ E_k \bigr)$$

上采样得到的特征语义强但边界模糊，编码器特征边界精细但语义弱，拼接之后，后面的卷积就能同时参考两者。

为什么小数据集也能训练

原论文的电子显微镜实验只有 30 张完整标注的训练图像。它主要依靠两点提高数据利用率：

  • 强数据增强：尤其是弹性形变，模拟组织的自然变形，让网络从很少的图像里学到更多变化。
  • 密集的像素级监督：每张标注图像都提供整幅分割图上的监督信号；这些像素并非彼此独立，但信息密度仍高于只给一个图像级标签。

跳跃连接的局限，以及后续改进

跳跃连接也有代价：编码器的浅层特征里混有大量背景噪声，直接拼接会把噪声一起送进解码器。

后续工作大多围绕这里展开：

改进方向 代表思路 对应内容
筛选跳跃特征 先用通道注意力给低层特征打权重再融合 MM-UNet 的 AFFM，原理同 SENet
更密集的连接 让特征在多级之间复用 DenseNet 的拼接思想
更强的全局建模 在瓶颈处加入 Transformer 或 Mamba MM-UNet 的 GCM
适配 3D 数据 3D 卷积，或各向异性卷积 MACM

从 2D 到 3D，再到 nnU-Net

  • 3D U-Net：把 2D 卷积和池化换成 3D 版本，直接处理 CT、MRI 体数据。代价是显存和参数量上升很快，可以对照 参数量 与 GFLOPs 估算。
  • nnU-Net：它的主要贡献不是新模块，而是一套能根据数据集自动确定预处理、网络深度、patch 大小和训练策略的流程。在很多医学分割基准上，一个调得好的 U-Net 仍是很强的基线。

实现时容易出错的地方

  1. 拼接前尺寸不一致：卷积不加 padding 时，编码器特征比解码器大，需要裁剪；现代实现通常用 same padding 避免这件事。
  2. 通道数写错：拼接后通道数是两者之和，下一个卷积的输入通道要相应修改。
  3. 归一化选择不合适：原论文没有 BatchNorm；现代实现常加入 BatchNorm 或 InstanceNorm，小 batch 的 3D 任务通常更适合考虑 InstanceNorm。
  4. 损失函数选择：前景占比很小时，单独的交叉熵容易被背景主导，通常配合 Dice Loss。
  5. 只看 Dice 就下结论：边界质量需要用 HD95 等指标 补充。

U-Net 的价值在于一种思路：先压缩得到理解，再把压缩前的细节带回来。后来大多数分割网络，都是在回答”带回来的细节该怎么挑”。

7 min read

Almost every medical segmentation paper starts from U-Net. It was not the first fully convolutional segmentation network, but it made pixel-accurate boundaries from a small dataset an ordinary thing. MM-UNet and most Transformer-based segmenters are this U-shaped skeleton with one part replaced or strengthened.

The problem it solves

Segmentation labels every pixel, and that needs two things that pull against each other:

  1. Knowing what it is — deep features with a large receptive field.
  2. Knowing where the boundary is — shallow, high-resolution features.

A classifier keeps downsampling and loses position. High-resolution features alone cannot see the whole object. U-Net’s answer: compress to gain meaning, expand to restore resolution, and send the pre-compression detail back through shortcuts.

Overall structure

The lavender path compresses space and learns semantics; the sage path restores resolution, while gold links return same-scale boundary detail to the decoder.

The three parts

Encoder (contracting path)

Each level has two 3x3 convolutions with ReLU, then 2x2 max pooling halves the spatial size while the channel count doubles. In the original paper channels grow from 64 to 1024 over four downsamplings.

As resolution drops, each position sees more of the image, and what it represents shifts from edges and texture to organs and regions.

Decoder (expanding path)

Each level upsamples (a 2x2 transposed convolution in the paper) to double the resolution and halve the channels, concatenates the encoder features of the same scale, and applies two 3x3 convolutions.

Skip connections

This is the key design. The output of encoder level k goes both to the next level and directly to the decoder at the same scale, joined along the channel axis:

$$\text{decoder input} = \mathrm{concat}\bigl( \mathrm{upsample}(F_{\text{deep}}),\ E_k \bigr)$$

Upsampled features are semantically strong but blurry at the edges; encoder features have sharp edges but little semantics. After concatenation the following convolutions can use both.

Why it trains on tiny datasets

The original paper had only a few dozen annotated images. It leaned on two things:

  • Heavy augmentation, especially elastic deformation, which mimics natural tissue deformation and squeezes more variety out of few images.
  • Every pixel is a training sample, so supervision per image is far denser than for image-level classification.

Limits of skip connections, and what followed

Shallow encoder features carry a lot of background noise, and concatenating them passes that noise on to the decoder.

Most later work targets exactly this:

Direction Idea See
Filter the skip features Weight low-level features with channel attention before fusing AFFM in MM-UNet, same principle as SENet
Denser connections Reuse features across many levels the concatenation idea in DenseNet
Stronger global modelling Put a Transformer or Mamba at the bottleneck GCM in MM-UNet
Fit 3D data 3D convolutions or anisotropic ones MACM

From 2D to 3D, and nnU-Net

  • 3D U-Net swaps 2D convolutions and pooling for 3D ones and works directly on CT and MRI volumes. Memory and parameters grow fast; compare parameter count and GFLOPs to estimate the cost.
  • nnU-Net‘s main contribution is not a new block but a pipeline that configures preprocessing, depth, patch size and training from the dataset itself. On many medical benchmarks a well-tuned U-Net is still a very strong baseline.

Where implementations go wrong

  1. Mismatched sizes before concatenation — without padding the encoder map is larger than the decoder’s and must be cropped; modern code uses same-padding to avoid this.
  2. Wrong channel counts — after concatenation the channels are the sum of both inputs, so the next convolution’s input must change.
  3. Forgetting normalisation — the paper had no BatchNorm; practical implementations add BatchNorm or, for small-batch 3D work, InstanceNorm.
  4. Choice of loss — with a small foreground, plain cross-entropy is dominated by background; it is usually paired with Dice Loss.
  5. Judging by Dice alone — boundary quality needs metrics such as HD95.

U-Net’s value is an idea: compress to understand, then bring back the detail you compressed away. Most later segmentation networks are answers to “which detail should come back?”
